About

I am Roman, a systems and platform engineer in Vienna. I have spent around ten years keeping business-critical systems alive in places where a bad afternoon costs real money and an audit finding costs even more: a manufacturer of radiotherapy devices in Salzburg, an Austrian IT service provider whose customers I looked after for eight years, and a group IT department. I never settled on one layer. I like the whole stack, from the hardware in the rack through storage and networking to the container platforms, and the monitoring that tells you something is wrong before a customer does.

I learned the trade by doing it. An apprenticeship at a hosting provider gave me my first production Linux box, and everything since has been production: incidents at odd hours, change windows, restores that had to work. That is where my habits come from. Change control and evidence are not paperwork to me, they are how I sleep at night. I prefer open source because I want to be able to read what I run, and I would rather operate something myself and understand it than rent it and hope.

Outside work I follow the EU rules that land on platforms, NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, and the AI Act, closely enough to have opinions about them. What ties it together is sovereignty: I want infrastructure that the people who depend on it can rebuild, audit, and run themselves. The environments that draw me are the ones where that matters most, defence, robotics, space, scientific and medical installations, and industrial systems, where the machine at the end of the network is real and the consequences are physical.

I have been a member of the IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society and the Computational Intelligence Society since 2013, which says something about what I read for fun: how systems regulate themselves, how they fail, and what it takes to keep a person meaningfully in the loop.

Open source

Some of what I build for the lab is public, because the habits are the point: a control plane that refuses to act without a plan, and the code that builds the hosts it operates.

relay-shell

Apache-2.0 · MCP

A Model Context Protocol server that lets an AI client work a real shell and SSH over hosts I administer: local and remote execution, interactive PTY sessions, SFTP, port forwarding, and parallel commands across a fleet, all through one audited runner. Authority is tiered (open, guarded, and read-only modes over a global deny-list), secrets are redacted, every call is recorded as a SHA-256 hash rather than a raw body, and everything is bounded by timeouts and output caps. I wanted an agent that could do real work without ever being trusted more than a new colleague on their first day.

infra

Apache-2.0 · OpenTofu

OpenTofu modules for the lab's hosts: cloud-init Ubuntu VMs on KVM/libvirt, and a Talos Linux module that stands up a Kubernetes cluster. Lab and production are kept apart, production state is configured for an encrypted, locked remote backend, and nothing merges without passing format, lint, security and secret scanning, and module tests against mock providers.

automation

Apache-2.0 · Ansible

Ansible for the same hosts: a CIS-benchmark-based hardening baseline covering SSH, auditd, firewalling, and kernel and systemd hardening, plus playbooks for the SRE toolchain, local inference, and out-of-band management. Controls map to NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, GDPR, and ISO/IEC 27001 in a machine-readable file, so the compliance story comes from the code rather than from a document sitting next to it.

The lab

I run a small fleet at home: a few Linux hosts on ZFS, a GPU box for local models, and a single-node Talos Kubernetes cluster whose state comes from Git alone. It started as a place to try the things I could not try at work. It has turned into the place where I think.

Most of it exists so I can read the news on my own terms. A PostgreSQL knowledge base pulls in some 130 OSINT and news feeds, holds over 220,000 documents with embeddings and some 38,000 CVEs enriched with KEV and EPSS, and answers questions through hybrid vector and full-text search with reranking. Local models on llama.cpp do the summarising, scheduled agents write me a briefing every morning, and the data lives on a machine at home rather than in someone else's cloud.

It is also where I practise what I preach. Every automated action, including the ones my AI tooling takes, goes through a plan, authorize, execute gate and lands in a hash-chained audit log. The hardening baseline is the automation repository above, applied to every host. Snapshots are scheduled and restores are tested rather than assumed. The Git that builds it all is self-hosted, so the lab depends on nothing I do not run. I break it regularly, on purpose and otherwise, and that is rather the point.
